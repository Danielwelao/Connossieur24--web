We've discussed what phishing is and broken down the mechanics behind fake security alerts. Now it's your turn to test your eye on a real-world scenario.

Take a close look at the email screenshot above. Don't just read what the message says—inspect **who sent it, when it was sent, what they are asking you to do, and where the link actually leads**.

How many red flags can you find before scrolling down?

## The Breakdown: 4 Red Flags Hidden in Plain Sight

Here is why this seemingly harmless project update is actually a phishing attempt:

### 1. The Sender Domain
* **What it shows:** The display name says "David Okafor," but the address is `david.okafor@gmail.com`.
* **The Red Flag:** Internal project updates and corporate documents rarely come from personal consumer `@gmail.com` addresses. Always verify that the sender's email domain matches your company's official domain.

### 2. The Link Destination (`drive--google.com`)
* **What it shows:** Hovering over the `[ OPEN DOCUMENT ]` link reveals the destination: `https://drive--google.com/file/d/...`.
* **The Red Flag:** Notice the double hyphen in `drive--google.com`. This is a typosquatting domain crafted to look like official Google Drive URLs (`drive.google.com`).

### 3. Late-Night Delivery with Immediate Urgency
* **What it shows:** The timestamp shows `10:47 PM`, paired with the request: *"I need your feedback before tomorrow morning."*
* **The Red Flag:** Pairing late-night delivery with a rush deadline is intended to catch you off-guard while tired, prompting you to click before verifying the details.

### 4. Vague Context
* **What it shows:** The body refers vaguely to "the project document we discussed earlier" without referencing a specific project name, file title, or team context.
* **The Red Flag:** Generic phrasing allows attackers to blast the same email template to hundreds of targets simultaneously.

## Key Takeaway

A familiar name doesn't mean a safe email. Always pause, hover over links to check the destination URL, and confirm unexpected file shares through a separate channel before logging in or entering credentials.

When we think of cybercrime, we often picture foreign corporations losing millions of dollars to sophisticated hacker groups. But the reality hits much closer to home. Cybercriminals are increasingly targeting local citizens, and the economic fallout is massive.

According to a comprehensive report on the economic cost of cybercrime in Nigeria, the threat is growing exponentially.

## A Multi-Trillion Naira Threat

![The Multi-Trillion Naira Threat](/october/day-28/2.jpeg)

The estimated consumer economic cost of cybercrime in Nigeria in a single year reached a staggering ₦2.14 Trillion. 

This is not money stolen from multinational banks or massive government entities. This is the financial impact absorbed by everyday consumers. It highlights a critical shift in cybercriminal strategy: as foreign targets become harder to breach, attackers are turning their focus inward toward the local population.

## The Impact Breakdown

![The Impact Breakdown](/october/day-28/3.jpeg)

The damage goes far beyond just stolen cash. The study revealed several alarming statistics about how deeply cybercrime affects individuals:

*   **High Victim Rate:** 41% of surveyed citizens reported falling victim to a cyberattack in the past. 
*   **Massive Time Loss:** Victims do not just lose money. Over 27,000 man-hours (equivalent to over 3 calendar years) were lost by respondents simply trying to recover from the incidents, restore accounts, and freeze compromised bank details.
*   **Data Compromise:** Of all the non-financial losses reported, 34% involved compromised personal data and effects. Financial information, government IDs, and private emails are routinely stolen and sold.

Cybercrime is not just a corporate problem. It is a deeply personal one. Everyday citizens are prime targets, and your everyday digital habits serve as your absolute first line of defense. Keep your guard up and secure your digital footprint.

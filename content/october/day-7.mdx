# The Human Side of Hacking: Social Engineering

![57% of adults worldwide experienced a scam in the past 12 months](/october/day-7/1.jpeg)

Not every cyberattack starts with a complicated piece of technology or sophisticated malware. What if an attack didn't start with a computer at all?

According to the **GASA & Feedzai Global State of Scams 2025** report, **57% of adults worldwide experienced a scam in the past 12 months**. Scams don't just exploit technology—they exploit people.

## What is Social Engineering?

![What is Social Engineering?](/october/day-7/2.jpeg)

Social engineering is the use of deception and psychological manipulation to influence people into revealing information, giving access, transferring money, or taking an action that benefits an attacker.

### The Target is the Person
Not every attack needs to break through a technical vulnerability, bypass a firewall, or crack a password. When systems are secured with strong technical defenses, attackers pivot to hacking the most unpredictable element in the security chain: **the human mind**.

## Common Psychological Triggers

Attackers use specific emotional levers to bypass your critical thinking and force quick action:

* **Urgency:** *"Your account will be suspended within 1 hour."* Creating time pressure so you act before you stop to think.
* **Fear:** *"Unusual sign-in detected. Verify now to prevent legal action."* Inducing anxiety to trigger immediate compliance.
* **Authority:** *"This is the CEO/IT Desk. I need your login credentials immediately for an urgent audit."* Impersonating figures of power so you don't question the request.
* **Trust:** *"Hey, a mutual friend shared your contact with me..."* Building false rapport or impersonating a colleague, bank, or friend.
* **Curiosity:** *"You won't believe what was posted about you in this document."* Drivng you to click suspicious links or download attachments without second-guessing.

## The Scammer's Goal

The ultimate objective of social engineering is simple: **get you to act before you stop to think.** 

Whether it's an email, a phone call, an SMS, or a direct message, understanding how these psychological levers are pulled is your first step to spotting the trap and protecting yourself.

## Key Takeaway

Always pause when a message triggers a strong emotional reaction—whether it's panic, excitement, or pressure to act immediately. Verify the request through an independent, trusted channel before sharing sensitive data or granting access.

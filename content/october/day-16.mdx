We've explored what identity theft is and how attackers hunt for your data. Now, it is time to focus on defense. 

The golden principle of digital safety is simple: you do not need to share everything about yourself to participate online. Guarding your data starts with everyday choices.

Share less. Secure more.

## The Defense Playbook

![The Defense Playbook](/october/day-16/2.jpeg)

You cannot control every risk on the internet, but you can massively reduce your exposure. Build these 6 habits to keep your identity locked down:

1.  **Passwords and MFA:** Use unique passphrases and turn on two-step verification for every important account.
2.  **Limit Oversharing:** Keep your birthdate, full name, and private documents off public social media feeds.
3.  **Verify Inquiries:** Always confirm requests for your personal data, even if the message looks like it is from a known organization.
4.  **Audit App Access:** Strip permissions from mobile apps that ask for your contacts, camera, or location without a clear reason.
5.  **Lock Down Hardware:** Set secure screen locks and enable remote wipe features on your phone and laptop.
6.  **Monitor Alerts:** Routinely review your bank transactions and keep an eye out for unexpected account sign-in notifications.

## What if Something Goes Wrong?

![Emergency Protocol](/october/day-16/3.jpeg)

If you suspect your identity has been compromised, speed is your best defense. Follow this basic incident protocol:

First, contact the affected bank or platform immediately using their official support channel. Do not click links in an alert email to reach out to them.

Second, change your credentials, force sign-out of all active sessions, and rotate your backup recovery codes.

Finally, flag any fraudulent transactions, document the event, and alert the appropriate authorities.

Act fast and stay in control of your identity.

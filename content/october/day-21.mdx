Every day, we share information online without thinking twice. We create new accounts, download mobile apps, shop for items, and post updates on social media. 

However, privacy is not just about keeping things secret. As the National Institute of Standards and Technology (NIST) states, privacy is about having the power to decide how your information is used. 

Privacy risks arise when your personal information is collected, shared, accessed, or weaponized in ways you did not expect or agree to.

## What Are You Giving Away?

![Privacy Risks Timeline](/october/day-21/2.jpeg)

Not every collection of data is harmful. The true risk comes when that information is exposed, misused, or shared without proper consent.

Every time you engage online, you leave a digital footprint that could include your:

1. **Location and Routines:** Tracking where you go, the places you frequently visit, and your daily physical patterns.
2. **Contact Info:** Sharing your primary phone number, email addresses, and the names of your personal connections.
3. **Photos and Posts:** Revealing sensitive life events, images of your home, and personal updates.
4. **Online Activity:** Exposing your browsing habits, shopping preferences, and private search history.
5. **Personal and Financial Data:** Putting your core identity, login credentials, and financial details at risk.

The more information you reveal online, the easier it becomes for someone to impersonate you, target you with highly convincing scams, or invade your privacy. 

Your personal information is highly valuable. Think carefully about where you share it and exactly who can access it. Think before you share.

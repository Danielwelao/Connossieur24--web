Data breaches do not always happen the way they are portrayed in movies. It is not always a dramatic hack by a hooded figure in a dark room. 

Sometimes, attackers do exploit complex technical weaknesses. But other times, a simple human mistake or an oversight exposes massive amounts of sensitive information to the public. 

## The Attack Vectors

![The Attack Vectors](/october/day-26/2.jpeg)

While every breach is unique, most unauthorized exposures stem from a few common vulnerabilities. Here are the five primary causes:

1. **Stolen Login Credentials:** Attackers use stolen, weak, or reused passwords to quietly access accounts without triggering alarms.
2. **Phishing Attacks:** Fake emails, messages, or websites trick people into voluntarily revealing their credentials or downloading malicious files.
3. **Unpatched Software:** Attackers actively exploit known vulnerabilities in outdated operating systems and applications.
4. **Misconfigured Systems:** Poor security settings, like leaving a cloud storage bucket open to the public, can accidentally expose private information.
5. **Insider Threats & Human Error:** Someone with authorized access may deliberately misuse it, send information to the wrong recipient, or expose data accidentally.

## The Ripple Effect

![The Ripple Effect](/october/day-26/3.jpeg)

Not every breach involves stolen passwords, and not every exposed record immediately leads to fraud. However, any unauthorized exposure deserves serious attention. 

Once data is breached, it often leads to a ripple effect of consequences, including identity theft, targeted phishing campaigns, total account takeovers, and a massive loss of trust for businesses.

Strong security depends on updated technology, continuous awareness, and the responsible handling of information.
